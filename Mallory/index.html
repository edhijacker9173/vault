    The unresolved case

    What the evidence can and cannot say

    01

    Odell’s “step”

    Odell first placed the pair at a prominent step high on the ridge. Whether this was the First, Second or Third Step changes the implied pace dramatically. His wording and identification evolved; the sighting remains real, its location contested.

    02

    The Second Step

    The crux rises near 8,610 metres. Modern reconstructions show it was climbable in period clothing, but not whether Mallory and Irvine climbed it that day—or whether they bypassed it through the Norton Couloir.

    03

    Camera & photograph

    Irvine probably carried a Kodak Vest Pocket camera. It has not been recovered. Mallory had promised to leave Ruth’s photograph on the summit; it was not among the effects found with him, but absence is not proof.

    04

    The fall

    Mallory’s injuries and waist rope mark suggest a roped fall. His goggles were found in a pocket, often read as evidence of descent after dark, though they may simply have been spare or removed in cloud.

    Responsible conclusion

    No summit can be confirmed.

    The evidence supports a high, determined attempt and a fatal fall. It does not establish whether either climber stood on the summit. Hillary and Tenzing’s 1953 ascent remains the first confirmed ascent.
